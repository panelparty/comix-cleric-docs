## Licensing Contibutions

By submitting a contribution to this project, you grant us a perpetual, irrevocable right to license your contribution under any terms, including commercial terms.

In simpler terms: By contributing documentation to this project, we have the right to license your contributions commercially (for instance, partnering with another indie hosting platform), without your explicit or written consent, and you cannot change your mind once the contribution as been accepted, as we will have you digitally sign-off on your submission.

This is very important, as you do own the rights to (almost) anything you contribute. By contributing, you are not waving your rights, but merely giving us express permission to sublicense it with the rest of our documentation. It keeps things easy and frictionless for everyone involved.

If you do not agree to these terms, you should not contribute to this codebase.

Also, this should go without saying, but you MUST own or have authorship rights to your contributions. We do not accept AI/LLM written or assisted documentation. Offending contributions will be removed, and you will be barred from future contributions entirely depending on the offense.

## Preparing

Clone this package to your local machine with `git`.

## Making Edits

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Please make sure that all provided information is accurate, relevant to the current documentation page, and simple to follow.

All of our documentation is "plain" Markdown. It makes use of GFM (GitHub-Flavored Markdown) to add special elements.

For example, alerts:

```md
> [!IMPORTANT]
> Please make sure that all provided information is accurate, relevant to the current documentation page, and simple to follow.
```

#### Keep Contibutions Within Project or Release Scope

This should go without saying, but documentation should not include features we do not yet support. Please keep all information relevant to the current release codebase.

If you are working on documentation for a future release, please keep it separate from the current version's documentation.

#### Generating changelogs

At this time, changelogs must be hand-crafted. At some time in the future, we may use a tool like `changeset` to generate them.

AI/LLM generated changelogs are not acceptable. No emojis, no em-dashes, no tell-tale signs of AI use, please. Even if you feel your written communication skills aren't great, outsourcing that to a machine doesn't help you or us because the AI is often entirely wrong and over-explains to the point of becoming just as difficult to understand. We encourage you to use your own words, even if you don't feel great about it. We will help with any grammatical errors, no judgement.
